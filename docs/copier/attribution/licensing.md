# Licensing

By default, anything you publish online, for example in a GitHub repository, is copyright by you. This is the default assumption, you own this product and you can share as you see fit. The licensing of your software is important to consider, especially if you are collaborating with others. It is also important to know, or understand the license of software you are using. For example, some licenses come with conditions that the product made using that software be under an open and permissive license.

**This is Critical** If you are using software packages, vendor products, or particular applications, you should understand the licenses you are using, and license your product accordingly. **Additionally** you should be aware that various funders and institutions have requirements for licensing products.

UBC has its own [standards for research data](https://researchdata.library.ubc.ca/deposit/license-your-data/) and [guidance for innovations and inventions generated at UBC](https://innovation.ubc.ca/resource-hub/ownership-inventions-ubc).

There are a large number of established licenses for software. Some good resources for understanding licenses include

* [Creative Commons License Chooser](https://creativecommons.org/chooser/)
* [The Legal Side of Open Source](https://opensource.guide/legal/)
* [Choose a License](https://choosealicense.com/)

The DERC [copier template](../using/index.md) provides three options that we consider to be the most appropriate for Open Science Software products. Each of these are open/permissive licenses, meaning your work may be freely used by others.

Your choice to participate in open scholarship is a personal one, and may be influenced by a number of factors. Although we recommend the open license, you are not obligated to use one, except where you are bound by university rules, funding requirements, or other obligations.
